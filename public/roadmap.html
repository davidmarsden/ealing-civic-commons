Public working roadmap · updated 29 August 2026

The prototype is working. The next job is to make it durable civic infrastructure.

Civic Commons has moved well beyond a read-only RSS experiment. It now combines discovery, moderated participation, account-free following, persistent civic memory, official-document monitoring and reviewed research context — while keeping original publishers and records canonical.

Where we are now

Four core layers are already live, with official-data and research integration actively expanding around them.

Discoverychronological local feeds, source health, provenance and public source submissions
Participationstable item pages, moderated contributions and reviewed public additions
Followingbrowser follows, portable personal RSS and double-opt-in email alerts
Memory15-minute item archiving, Document Watch and reviewed civic relationships

The plan

Eight phases, now grounded in what is actually live.

The earlier roadmap split the RSS/social prototype from the later research graph. That no longer reflects the product. This version treats them as one system: current civic information, participation, durable evidence and reviewed context all share stable identities and visible provenance.

Phase 1
Baseline complete

Read-only civic aggregation

Multiple local journalism, community, civic and official feeds are normalised into one chronological Commons timeline with source health, filters, canonical links and public source submissions. Source expansion and ingestion hardening continue, but the baseline product is proven.

Phase 2
Complete v1

Stable civic items & moderated participation

Items have durable Commons URLs and thread identities. Residents can submit corrections, evidence, related sources, local information and context for human review; approved additions are published with provenance rather than engagement ranking.

Phase 3
Complete v1

Follow, RSS & email delivery

Stories, sources, places and topics can be followed without an account. The same stable follow model powers the Following view, portable personal RSS and double-opt-in email alerts. No behavioural profiling or engagement ranking is required.

Phase 4
Core live

Persistent civic memory

Normalised items are archived every 15 minutes in a persistent store. Stable item pages can survive RSS expiry, directly followed stories remain resolvable, and the full Document Watch stream is retained even when routine records are not promoted to the main timeline.

Phase 5
Active · major v1 live

Official publishing & Document Watch

Ealing Council news and category feeds now enrich the Commons, 47 council download feeds are registered, and Document Watch has its own browsable section with human-readable document descriptions and collection/topic filtering. ModernGov access and OCN integration continue in parallel, not as blockers.

Phase 6
Core live

Reviewed civic graph & research context

People, organisations, places, topics and issues have first-class Commons identities. Reviewed relationships, primary evidence and Southall Stories research context can be connected to live civic items while keeping source provenance explicit.

Phase 7
Next major phase

Structured review & community knowledge

Turn the current manual moderation workflow into a durable review queue with pending/accepted/rejected states, reviewer notes and an audit trail. Community input can propose corrections, evidence and relationships, but never silently rewrite reviewed civic knowledge.

Phase 8
Partly live

Automation, search & resilience

Archiving, alerts and research exports are already automated. The remaining infrastructure work is archive indexing/search, version handling, parser/caching hardening, source-health tooling, automatic preservation of new research material and stronger link-rot resilience.

Immediate next slice

Consolidate before expanding again.

  1. Build the Phase 7 review queue so moderated participation can scale beyond a manual JSON publication workflow.
  2. Index persistent civic items for archive/search views and richer historical follows.
  3. Connect Document Watch to civic memory by relating selected primary documents to issues, entities and earlier reporting.
  4. Strengthen source infrastructure with more registry-driven configuration, parser tests, caching and source-health tooling.
  5. Continue ModernGov and OCN work in parallel, without allowing either external dependency to block the Commons.
The boundary still matters. Original publishers and official records remain canonical. Provenance stays visible. Participation is moderated, not popularity-ranked. External social systems may connect later, but Civic Commons keeps its own public URLs, thread identities, subscriptions, reviewed contributions and civic relationships.

The implementation-level roadmap now lives with the project itself: docs/ROADMAP.md. The older Southall-Zettel roadmap remains useful for the research corpus, but it is no longer the master roadmap for Civic Commons.
