Public working roadmap · updated 5 September 2026

The prototype era is over. Next: connect the evidence and make the Commons scale.

Civic Commons now combines discovery, moderated participation, account-free following, persistent civic memory, official-document monitoring, Open Council Network meeting summaries, ModernGov publication events, reviewed research context and a complete contributor feedback loop — while keeping original publishers and records canonical. Ealing Civic Commons is the first local implementation within the wider Civic Commons network.

Where we are now

Phases 1–3 are complete, persistent memory and official publishing have substantial working versions, and the reviewed civic graph and moderation membrane are live. The next work is structured promotion, stronger search/indexing and sustainable source operations.

The plan

Eight phases, grounded in what is actually live.

The Commons treats current civic information, participation, durable evidence and reviewed context as one system. The central rule is simple: suggested material does not become reviewed civic knowledge merely because it was submitted, discovered or generated. Promotion remains an explicit human decision with provenance.

Phase 1
Complete

Read-only civic aggregation

The original foundation is complete: local journalism, official records, RSS/Atom, YouTube/video, filtered London-wide material, community organisations and monitored public pages converge on one chronological provenance-rich stream, with seven-town/topic/source-class filtering, visible source health, source submissions, Ealing Council publishing, Document Watch and direct Open Council Network public Ealing summaries. Recent additions include Stop The Towers, Friends of the Victoria Hall, Southall Speaks, Visit Southall and Vicious Ealing Council. Native feeds, source-specific first-party page adapters and cautious bridge/fallback patterns are now all established ingestion routes.

Phase 2
Complete v1

Stable civic items & moderated participation

Items have durable Commons URLs and thread identities. Residents can submit corrections, evidence, related sources, local information and context for human review; approved additions are published with provenance rather than engagement ranking.

Phase 3
Complete v1

Follow, RSS & email delivery

Stories, sources, places and topics can be followed without an account. Browser follows, portable personal RSS and double-opt-in email alerts share the same civic source universe.

Phase 4
Major v1 live

Persistent civic memory & Archive

Normalised items are archived every 15 minutes and remain available after RSS expiry. The browsable Civic Archive supports search, source, place and topic filtering, while reviewed new context can resurface an older story as fresh Commons activity without changing its original publication date or publisher.

Phase 5
Active · major v1 live

Official publishing & Document Watch

Ealing Council feeds, 47 registered council document collections, London Assembly/City Hall material, official video and Ealing-filtered Met sources feed the same civic pipeline. OCN’s public Ealing meeting summaries are integrated through a conservative bridge, and ModernGov agenda, minutes, decision, issue, plan and ePetition updates arrive from the official feed through a public feed-reader bridge. The remaining work is enrichment: better direct public destinations, meeting/document relationships and preservation of high-value evidence.

Phase 6
Core live

Reviewed civic graph & research context

People, organisations, places, topics and issues have first-class Commons identities. Curated research adds reviewed relationships, descriptions and source links. The continuing housekeeping target is the identity + description + provenance + source standard for every public entity.

Phase 7
7A + 7B complete · 7C next

Structured review & civic knowledge

7A delivered the durable private review queue, moderation states and audit trail. 7B publishes accepted story contributions automatically, keeps accepted/published states distinct, supports safe withdrawal/retry, emails contributors on meaningful decisions and provides private bookmarkable status pages. 7C will extend deliberate promotion rules to source submissions, evidence suggestions and relationship suggestions — one type at a time.

Phase 8
Partly live

Automation, search & resilience

Archiving, alerts, review persistence, research exports and official/source-specific adapters are already automated. Public source health uses plain-language status and is alphabetised for scanning, while technical fetch diagnostics are hidden from ordinary readers and available with ?debug=1. Remaining work includes scalable archive indexing, registry-driven source configuration, grouped/searchable source facets, parser/caching hardening, safer version handling and automatic completeness checks.

Town views
Major v1 partly live

One Commons, seven town identities

Ealing, Acton, Greenford, Hanwell, Northolt, Perivale and Southall have coherent town marks and town-aware views without splitting the underlying civic data into seven separate sites. Southall can be entered through its dedicated Commons subdomain while navigation remains connected both to the borough-wide Ealing Civic Commons and to the wider Civic Commons network front door. The same rule applies to any future local or borough subdomain: local entry points must never become navigational dead ends. Town share-card assets exist, but item and entity routes still need the social-metadata renderer restored before those cards reliably appear when public URLs are shared.

Immediate next slice

Turn accepted evidence into connected civic memory.

  1. Build Phase 7C promotion rules for accepted source submissions and evidence suggestions first, then relationship suggestions.
  2. Connect accepted evidence to civic memory: archived stories, Document Watch records, issues, entities and earlier reporting, without copying or displacing the canonical source.
  3. Scale Archive indexing and search so a rapidly growing source universe stays fast and complete rather than relying indefinitely on whole-store scans.
  4. Make source filtering scale with searchable/grouped source families as dozens of source identities become hundreds.
  5. Finish the entity completeness audit and automate checks for newly incomplete records.
  6. Harden and extend official-source ingestion, especially ModernGov direct links, meeting/document relationships, parser tests, caching and preservation of high-value primary records.
  7. Finish town-aware sharing by restoring metadata rendering for item/entity URLs so the existing town share-card assets are actually emitted to social crawlers.
  8. Continue source expansion by genuine geographic and thematic gap while hardening current adapters. Positive Greenford is a promising active Greenford source; Northolt and especially Perivale still need stronger first-party publishing. OCN partnership/API work can continue in parallel without becoming a blocker.
The boundary still matters. Original publishers and official records remain canonical. Provenance stays visible, but public wording should be understandable without knowing the ingestion architecture. A bare name is not a finished civic entity. Historical and current organisations are framed in time rather than flattened together. Participation and civic assertions are moderated, not popularity-ranked. Private submitter data remains private. External social systems may connect later, but Civic Commons keeps its own public URLs, subscriptions, reviewed contributions and civic relationships.
