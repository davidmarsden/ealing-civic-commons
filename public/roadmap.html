Public working roadmap

Building a civic memory for Southall and Ealing.

The Civic Commons already connects current local reporting and public records. The next layer connects today’s items to reviewed historical context, people, organisations, places and primary evidence — without taking ownership away from the original sources.

Where we are now

The Southall Stories research archive is already feeding a reviewed civic-memory layer into the Commons. The underlying graph currently contains:

115curated people, organisations and places
33curated primary or authoritative source records
25reviewed entity-to-entity relationships
1,898graph relationships in the current research layer

The plan

Eight phases, one open system.

This roadmap is intentionally public. The research archive remains the deep review surface; the Civic Commons consumes a safe, provenance-rich subset for discovery and historical context. Generated suggestions can help researchers find connections, but editorial assertions are reviewed before they become part of the public graph.

Phase 1
Complete for baseline

Candidate entity discovery

Automatically surface likely people, organisations and places from the preserved Southall Stories corpus, then require human review before promotion into the curated graph.

Phase 2
Active

Reviewed relationships

Build explicit, evidence-backed relationships between curated entities — for example leadership, development, employment, regulation and location — while keeping them distinct from mere co-occurrence.

Phase 3
Active

Primary evidence records

Promote important council decisions, scrutiny reports, regulatory material, planning documents and other authoritative evidence into stable local source records with provenance and backlinks.

Phase 4
Complete v1

Public Civic Commons export

Publish a stable, versioned subset of reviewed civic-memory data that Commons can consume without depending on the research archive’s internal file structure.

Phase 5
Live

Related civic memory on stories

Southall Stories items in the Commons can now show related people, organisations, places, topics, earlier reporting and selected evidence. If enrichment is unavailable, the normal Commons page still works.

Phase 6
Next

First-class entity and issue pages

Create public Commons pages for places, organisations, people and civic issues. The first pilot will use a rich, well-evidenced subject such as Southall Gasworks, combining current material from multiple publishers with reviewed historical context.

Phase 7
Later

Suggestions back into review

Let residents, sources and the Commons itself suggest evidence or relationships, with provenance and an audit trail. Suggestions remain pending until reviewed; community input never silently changes curated research.

Phase 8
Partly live

Sustainable automation

Close the loop: automatically preserve new Southall Stories material, rebuild the research graph and public export, refresh Commons enrichment and flag ambiguous changes for human review.

Immediate next slice

From memory cards to civic pages.

  1. Keep deepening reviewed relationships and primary evidence in coherent evidence clusters.
  2. Build the Phase 6 pilot: a first-class Commons page for Southall Gasworks.
  3. Generalise the page model to other places, organisations, people and issues.
  4. Then build the participation/review queue and finish the automatic ingestion loop.
The boundary matters. Sources keep ownership of their journalism and records. The research archive connects and reviews evidence; Civic Commons presents a deliberately public subset. Community suggestions flow back only as review candidates.
