Public working roadmap · updated 30 August 2026

The prototype is working. The next job is to make it durable civic infrastructure.

Civic Commons now combines discovery, moderated participation, account-free following, persistent civic memory, official-document and video monitoring, and reviewed research context — while keeping original publishers and records canonical.

Where we are now

Four core layers are already live, with official-data, community-source and research integration actively expanding around them.

Discoverylocal feeds, video, monitored public pages, source health, provenance and submissions
Participationstable item pages, moderated contributions and reviewed public additions
Followingbrowser follows, portable personal RSS and double-opt-in email alerts
Memory15-minute item archiving, Document Watch and reviewed civic relationships

The plan

Eight phases, grounded in what is actually live.

The Commons now treats current civic information, participation, durable evidence and reviewed context as one system. A new housekeeping rule applies across the graph: a bare name is not a finished entity. Public identities need a useful description, visible provenance and a first-party or authoritative source/website where one exists.

Phase 1
Baseline complete

Read-only civic aggregation

Local journalism, official records, YouTube/video, filtered London-wide material, community organisations, faith groups, education and monitored public pages can now converge on one chronological Commons stream. Source expansion continues deliberately, with civic relevance and first-party provenance preferred over indiscriminate aggregation.

Phase 2
Complete v1

Stable civic items & moderated participation

Items have durable Commons URLs and thread identities. Residents can submit corrections, evidence, related sources, local information and context for human review; approved additions are published with provenance rather than engagement ranking.

Phase 3
Complete v1

Follow, RSS & email delivery

Stories, sources, places and topics can be followed without an account. The public timeline, personal RSS and email-alert chain now share the same combined source universe, avoiding gaps between what can be discovered and what can be followed.

Phase 4
Core live

Persistent civic memory

Normalised items are archived every 15 minutes. Stable item pages can survive RSS expiry, Document Watch is retained, and living-publication monitors preserve meaningful public-page changes without inventing publication dates or fake article URLs.

Phase 5
Active · major v1 live

Official publishing & Document Watch

Ealing Council feeds, Document Watch, London Assembly/City Hall sources, official video and Ealing-filtered Met material now feed the same civic pipeline. Next comes stronger document search and transcript/caption enrichment while keeping original records and videos canonical.

Phase 6
Core live

Reviewed civic graph & research context

People, organisations, places, topics and issues have first-class Commons identities. Entity descriptions now flow from curated research notes, Explore can show source/website links, and current community, faith, political and institutional identities can sit alongside clearly framed historical entities. The immediate housekeeping target is to bring every public entity up to the identity + description + provenance + source standard.

Phase 7
Next major phase

Structured review & community knowledge

Turn the current moderation workflow into a durable review queue with pending/accepted/rejected states, reviewer notes and an audit trail. Community — and later private research — can propose evidence and relationships, but no candidate becomes reviewed knowledge or a finished entity without human review and the entity-completeness standard.

Phase 8
Partly live

Automation, search & resilience

Archiving, alerts and research exports are already automated. Remaining work includes archive search, source-health tooling, parser/caching hardening, safer version handling, automatic research ingestion and automated checks for incomplete entity records.

Immediate next slice

Consolidate the graph while source coverage keeps growing.

  1. Finish the entity completeness audit: every public entity gets a useful description and appropriate source/website link, with historical/current framing made explicit.
  2. Continue deliberate source expansion across faith/community organisations, education, legal advice, campaigning and local politics.
  3. Build the Phase 7 review queue so moderated participation — and later private-research candidates — has a durable destination.
  4. Index persistent civic items for archive/search views and richer historical follows.
  5. Connect Document Watch and video to civic memory through primary documents, transcripts and reviewed entity/issue links.
  6. Strengthen source infrastructure with registry-driven configuration, parser tests, caching and source-health tooling.
The boundary still matters. Original publishers and official records remain canonical. Provenance stays visible. A bare name is not a finished civic entity. Historical and current organisations are framed in time rather than flattened together. Participation is moderated, not popularity-ranked. External social systems may connect later, but Civic Commons keeps its own public URLs, subscriptions, reviewed contributions and civic relationships.

The implementation-level roadmap lives with the project itself: docs/ROADMAP.md. The research-graph roadmap remains in the Southall Stories research archive issue tracker and now carries the same entity-completeness rule.
