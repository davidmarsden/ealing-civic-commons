Public working roadmap

Building a civic memory for Southall and Ealing.

Civic Commons now connects current local material with reviewed historical context, people, organisations, places, topics, ongoing issues and primary evidence — while keeping the original sources and their provenance visible.

Where we are now

The Southall Stories research archive supplies one reviewed layer, while Civic Commons now also owns public civic identities, topics, issues and its first independently reviewed assertions. The current research layer contains:

115curated people, organisations and places in the research archive
33curated primary or authoritative source records
25reviewed archive entity-to-entity relationships
1,888graph relationships in the current research layer

The plan

Eight phases, one open system.

This roadmap is intentionally public. The research archive remains a deep review surface; Civic Commons consumes a safe, provenance-rich subset and can now add its own reviewed knowledge alongside it. Generated suggestions can help researchers find connections, but editorial assertions are reviewed before they become part of the public graph.

Phase 1
Complete for baseline

Candidate entity discovery

Automatically surface likely people, organisations and places from the preserved Southall Stories corpus, then require human review before promotion into the curated graph. The current baseline queue is empty at the configured threshold.

Phase 2
Active

Reviewed relationships

Build explicit, evidence-backed relationships between curated entities — for example leadership, development, employment, regulation and location — while keeping them distinct from mere co-occurrence. The priority now is deeper evidence clusters and useful temporal roles.

Phase 3
Active

Primary evidence records

Promote important council decisions, scrutiny reports, regulatory material, planning documents and other authoritative evidence into stable source records with provenance and backlinks, then deepen preservation and link-rot resilience over time.

Phase 4
Complete v1

Public Civic Commons export

Publish a stable, versioned subset of reviewed civic-memory data that Commons can consume without depending on the research archive’s internal file structure.

Phase 5
Live

Related civic memory on stories

Southall Stories items in the Commons can show related people, organisations, places, topics, earlier reporting and selected evidence. If enrichment is unavailable, the normal Commons page still works.

Phase 6
Core live

First-class civic graph pages

People, organisations and places now have canonical Commons pages; Explore provides a browsable graph; ten civic topic pages are live; Southall Gasworks redevelopment is the first ongoing issue page; and Commons-native reviewed assertions can sit alongside research-archive evidence on the same civic object. The remaining work here is refinement and deeper multi-provider coverage, not proving the page architecture.

Phase 7
Next

Suggestions back into review

Let residents, sources and the Commons itself suggest evidence or relationships, with provenance, pending/accepted/rejected states and an audit trail. Community input must never silently change reviewed knowledge.

Phase 8
Partly live

Sustainable automation

Graph rebuilds, candidate discovery and public exports are automated after curated changes. The remaining loop is automatic preservation of new or changed Southall Stories material, ambiguity review, stronger refresh/version handling and clean propagation into Commons.

Immediate next slice

From working graph to sustainable civic infrastructure.

  1. Polish the live Phase 6 surfaces: keep terminology human-readable, strengthen navigation between entities/topics/issues and add more independently reviewed Commons knowledge where it is useful.
  2. Deepen Phases 2–3 in coherent evidence clusters, beginning with a strong Ealing governance/political-power cluster rather than loose one-off relationships.
  3. Build Phase 7: a structured suggestions and review queue with explicit provenance and an audit trail.
  4. Close Phase 8 by detecting and preserving new Southall Stories material automatically, rebuilding the graph and safely refreshing Commons.
The boundary matters. Sources keep ownership of their journalism and records. Reviewed evidence can arrive from several providers, but Civic Commons keeps their provenance visible. Community suggestions flow back only as review candidates.
