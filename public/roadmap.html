Ealing reference implementation · updated 8 September 2026

Planning is now part of civic memory. Next: deepen the connections.

Ealing Civic Commons now connects live local publishing, reviewed civic memory and official planning records around durable places and issues. The wider Civic Commons network, Charter of the Commons and Start a Civic Commons guide remain separate from this Ealing implementation roadmap.

Where we are now

Discovery, participation, following, persistent memory, official publishing, planning records, review and the civic graph all have substantial working versions. The newest milestone is a public planning layer that links applications into place and issue history while keeping Ealing Council's register canonical.

September milestone

The weekly planning list became place memory.

Ealing PAM planning applications now have stable Commons objects. Conservative town links and deliberately reviewed site links connect them to place pages, and issues can inherit planning through a reviewed primary place. The first live example is application 263308CND at 2 The Straight, connected to Southall, Southall Gasworks and the Southall Gasworks redevelopment issue.

The public site reads a validated committed snapshot rather than scraping PAM on page load. Partial ingests fail closed. Documents, drawings and mapping tiles are not mirrored. The council register remains authoritative.

The plan

Eight implementation phases, plus a network layer.

Ealing treats current civic information, participation, durable evidence and reviewed context as one system. Places are now an explicit long-lived organising unit: a place can accumulate current facts, local evidence, recent Commons material, planning records, primary evidence, historical reporting and reviewed relationships without flattening their provenance.

Phase 1
Complete

Read-only civic aggregation

Local journalism, official records, RSS/Atom, video, community publishing and monitored public pages converge on one chronological provenance-rich stream, with town/topic/source filtering and visible source health.

Phase 2
Complete v1

Stable civic items & moderated participation

Items have durable Commons URLs and thread identities. Corrections, evidence and context can be submitted for review and published without engagement ranking.

Phase 3
Complete v1

Follow, RSS & email delivery

Stories, sources, places and topics can be followed without an account through browser follows, personal RSS and double-opt-in email.

Phase 4
Major v1 live

Persistent civic memory & Archive

Normalised items persist beyond feed expiry in the Civic Archive. Entity/place/issue dossiers now present current context first and deeper evidence/history on demand.

Phase 5
Active · major v1 live

Official publishing, Document Watch & planning

Ealing Council publishing, ModernGov, OCN summaries, Document Watch and now PAM planning applications feed the same civic layer while preserving official sources as canonical. Next: planning decisions, ward/site geography and richer links to democratic records.

Phase 6
Core live

Reviewed civic graph & place memory

People, organisations, places, topics and issues have first-class Commons identities. Planning-to-place relationships now carry explicit provenance, and issues can inherit planning through reviewed primary places.

Phase 7
7A + 7B complete · 7C next

Structured review & civic knowledge

The durable review queue, publication flow and contributor feedback are live. Phase 7C extends deliberate promotion rules to source, evidence and relationship suggestions.

Phase 8
Partly live

Automation, search & resilience

Archiving, alerts, research exports, source adapters and planning snapshot refresh are automated. Remaining work includes scalable indexing, parser regression tests, completeness checks and safer version handling.

Town views
Major v1 partly live

One Ealing Commons, seven town identities

Ealing, Acton, Greenford, Hanwell, Northolt, Perivale and Southall have local marks and town-aware views without splitting the underlying civic data.

Network
Foundation live

Charter, network identity & reproducibility

civiccommons.co.uk is the network front door. The Charter defines shared principles while Start a Civic Commons explains how another locality can build its own version.

Immediate next slice

Deepen planning and evidence around durable civic places.

  1. Extend planning lifecycle memory with decisions/status history, reliable ward/site geography and related applications.
  2. Connect planning to democratic context: committees, decisions, documents and relevant reporting where deterministic or reviewed.
  3. Build Phase 7C promotion rules for accepted source submissions and evidence suggestions first, then relationship suggestions.
  4. Scale Archive indexing/search as the source and memory universe grows.
  5. Finish the public entity completeness audit and automate checks for newly incomplete records.
  6. Harden official-source ingestion, including ModernGov links, planning parser tests and caching.
  7. Finish town-aware sharing for stable public routes.
  8. Continue source expansion by genuine geographic/thematic gap, especially Perivale.
The boundary still matters. Original publishers and official registers remain canonical. A public-register name is not automatically a civic profile. Specific site relationships are explicit and reviewed rather than fuzzily inferred. Places are durable civic-memory objects; feeds and weekly lists are transient inputs. Provenance stays visible. Chronology is not replaced by engagement ranking. Private submitter data remains private. No behavioural advertising. No compulsory closed-platform identity.
