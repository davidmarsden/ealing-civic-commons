Ealing reference implementation · updated 6 September 2026

The prototype era is over. Next: connect the evidence and make the Commons scale.

Ealing Civic Commons is now explicitly the first local implementation in the wider Civic Commons network. The network-level Charter of the Commons and Start a Civic Commons field guide live on the top-level domain; this roadmap records what is live and next in Ealing.

Where we are now

Discovery, participation, following, persistent memory, official publishing, review and the civic graph all have substantial working versions. The live source set now also includes EALING.NEWS, LAGER Can, the four main local party organisations, Hanwell Community Forum, Southall Community Alliance, Norwood Green Residents’ Association and Bedford Park Society. The network model is documented separately from the Ealing implementation. The next work is structured promotion, stronger search/indexing and sustainable source operations.

The plan

Eight implementation phases, plus a network layer.

Ealing treats current civic information, participation, durable evidence and reviewed context as one system. The constitutional rule remains simple: suggested material does not become reviewed civic knowledge merely because it was submitted, discovered or generated. The shared principles now live in the Charter; Ealing remains the working laboratory.

Phase 1
Complete

Read-only civic aggregation

Local journalism, official records, RSS/Atom, video, filtered London-wide material, community and residents’ organisations, local political publishing and monitored public pages converge on one chronological provenance-rich stream, with seven-town/topic/source-class filtering, visible source health, source submissions, Ealing Council publishing, Document Watch, OCN summaries and ModernGov publication events.

Phase 2
Complete v1

Stable civic items & moderated participation

Items have durable Commons URLs and thread identities. Residents can submit corrections, evidence, related sources, local information and context for human review; approved additions are published with provenance rather than engagement ranking.

Phase 3
Complete v1

Follow, RSS & email delivery

Stories, sources, places and topics can be followed without an account. Browser follows, portable personal RSS and double-opt-in email alerts share the same civic source universe.

Phase 4
Major v1 live

Persistent civic memory & Archive

Normalised items are archived every 15 minutes and remain available after RSS expiry. The Civic Archive supports search, source, place and topic filtering, while reviewed context can reactivate older records without rewriting their original publication history.

Phase 5
Active · major v1 live

Official publishing & Document Watch

Ealing Council feeds and document collections, London Assembly/City Hall material, filtered Met sources, OCN summaries and ModernGov updates feed the same civic pipeline. Remaining work is enrichment, stronger relationships and preservation of high-value evidence.

Phase 6
Core live

Reviewed civic graph & research context

People, organisations, places, topics and issues have first-class Commons identities. Curated research adds reviewed relationships, descriptions and source links. The continuing target is identity + description + provenance + source for every public entity.

Phase 7
7A + 7B complete · 7C next

Structured review & civic knowledge

The durable review queue, moderation states, publication flow and contributor feedback are live. Phase 7C will extend deliberate promotion rules to source submissions, evidence suggestions and relationship suggestions one type at a time.

Phase 8
Partly live

Automation, search & resilience

Archiving, alerts, review persistence, research exports and source-specific adapters are automated. Remaining work includes scalable archive indexing, registry-driven source configuration, grouped/searchable source facets, parser/caching hardening, version handling and automatic completeness checks.

Town views
Major v1 partly live

One Ealing Commons, seven town identities

Ealing, Acton, Greenford, Hanwell, Northolt, Perivale and Southall have local marks and town-aware views without splitting the data into seven sites. Southall remains a doorway into the canonical Ealing Commons. Every local doorway must retain visible navigation both to Ealing and to the wider Civic Commons network.

Network
Foundation live

Charter, network identity & reproducibility

civiccommons.co.uk is now the network front door with the Open Gate identity. The Charter of the Commons defines shared principles, while Start a Civic Commons explains how another locality can build its own version. Ealing's roadmap and founding documents are explicitly presented as reference implementation material, not franchise rules.

Immediate next slice

Turn accepted evidence into connected civic memory.

  1. Build Phase 7C promotion rules for accepted source submissions and evidence suggestions first, then relationship suggestions.
  2. Connect accepted evidence to civic memory: archived stories, Document Watch records, issues, entities and earlier reporting.
  3. Scale Archive indexing and search so growth from dozens of sources to hundreds remains fast and complete.
  4. Make source filtering scale with searchable/grouped source families.
  5. Finish the entity completeness audit and automate checks for newly incomplete records.
  6. Harden official-source ingestion, especially ModernGov direct links, meeting/document relationships, parser tests and caching.
  7. Finish town-aware sharing so the existing town share-card assets are emitted to social crawlers.
  8. Keep expanding by genuine geographic gap, not raw source count: Greenford and Northolt now have live first-party sources and Hanwell has improved, while Perivale remains the clearest publishing gap. Keep fragile upstreams visible through source health rather than silently dropping them.
  9. Keep the reference implementation reproducible by updating public docs as Ealing evolves and feeding genuinely reusable lessons back into the network guide rather than silently turning Ealing choices into network requirements.
The boundary still matters. Original publishers and official records remain canonical. Provenance stays visible. Chronology is not replaced by engagement ranking. Participation and civic assertions are moderated. Private submitter data remains private. No behavioural advertising. No compulsory closed-platform identity. Ealing may inform the wider model, but the network remains a network, not a franchise.
