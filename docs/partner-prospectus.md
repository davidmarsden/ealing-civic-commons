# Southall & Ealing Civic Commons
## Partner Prospectus

**An open civic information network, beginning in Southall**

### The proposition

Local democracy produces an extraordinary amount of public information. The problem is not simply that information is unavailable. It is that it is scattered.

A resident trying to understand a development, council decision, environmental issue or community campaign may need to move between council committee pages, PDFs, local news websites, residents' groups, newsletters, campaign sites, social media, public datasets and historic archives.

Each source may contain an important piece of the story. Almost nothing joins the pieces together.

**Southall & Ealing Civic Commons proposes to connect them.**

The project would begin in Southall and be designed to expand across Ealing's seven distinct towns and areas: **Acton, Ealing, Greenford, Hanwell, Northolt, Perivale and Southall**.

A future borough-wide service should not assume that the priorities of **Ealing, Southall, Greenford, Acton, Northolt, Perivale or Hanwell are interchangeable**.

It would not ask publishers or organisations to surrender their websites to a new platform. Instead, open feeds and structured public data would allow independently published material to appear together in a simple local civic timeline.

> **Publish anywhere. Connect locally. Keep the sources open.**

### What might somebody see?

Imagine a planning decision affecting Southall.

Today, the relevant information might be divided between an Ealing Council agenda, an officer's report, Open Council Network's meeting record, local newspaper coverage, an investigation by Southall Stories, a residents' association statement and discussion among residents.

The Civic Commons would connect those materials around the same civic event while preserving their identities.

**OFFICIAL RECORD**  
Ealing Council — agenda, report, decision and minutes

**INDEPENDENT CIVIC RECORD**  
Open Council Network — structured meeting information and source-linked interpretation

**LOCAL REPORTING**  
Independent local journalism and analysis

**COMMUNITY VOICES**  
Residents' organisations, campaigns and local publishers

Every item would link back to its original source.

The purpose is not to tell residents which voice to believe. It is to make it dramatically easier to discover the evidence, understand the context and encounter relevant local perspectives.

### Why not simply use social media?

Social networks are useful distribution channels, but poor civic infrastructure.

Important local information competes with everything else in algorithmic feeds. Historic discussion is difficult to recover. Publishers and communities become dependent on private platforms whose rules and interfaces can change without notice.

The Commons would use open technologies such as RSS, Atom, APIs and portable subscription formats underneath an ordinary public-facing website.

Users would not need to know what any of those technologies are.

Bluesky, Mastodon, Facebook or future networks could distribute links into the Commons, but none would own the underlying civic information layer.

### We can already build a meaningful pilot

The initial source census has identified a surprisingly strong open publishing ecosystem.

Candidate sources already include:

- Southall Stories;
- Southall Residents Alliance;
- MySouthall;
- EALING.NEWS;
- Asian Standard's Southall coverage;
- Ealing Matters;
- The View from W5;
- West Ealing Neighbours;
- Ealing Council's official ModernGov publishing feed;
- Open Council Network.

Several already expose standard RSS/XML feeds and can be connected without asking them to adopt new software.

Ealing Council's ModernGov system provides an official stream of newly published democratic material. That means a genuinely useful prototype can be built before introducing accounts, comments or any new social network.

### Why Open Council Network matters

Open Council Network could provide the bridge between a chronological aggregator and something substantially more useful: a structured map of local democratic activity.

Its data can potentially give the Commons consistent representations of meetings, committees, documents, people and topics. Those stable civic entities can then be connected with journalism and community publishing.

The projects are complementary.

Open Council Network tackles the difficult problem of making local-government information structured and understandable at scale.

The Civic Commons asks:

> **What becomes possible when that structured democratic record is connected to the information ecosystem of one real community?**

We would like to explore Ealing — beginning with Southall — as a public-interest pilot or development partnership.

Areas for discussion include:
- appropriate Ealing API access;
- stable meeting/document identifiers;
- provenance and attribution;
- presentation of AI-assisted summaries alongside primary records;
- caching/reuse terms;
- co-design of neighbourhood-level civic workflows;
- whether lessons from the pilot could be reproduced elsewhere.

### Trust has to be designed in

The Civic Commons would be independent of Ealing Council, political parties, elected representatives, commercial developers and any single publisher.

Southall Stories may help establish it, but should not permanently own the civic space.

The interface would clearly distinguish:
- official records;
- independent structured information;
- journalism;
- organisational/campaign material;
- community contributions;
- Commons-generated classification or summaries.

AI may eventually help classify, translate, match or summarise material, but must never erase provenance. People should always be able to ask: **where did this come from?**

Political neutrality would not mean neutrality about democratic values. The founding charter supports access to evidence, freedom of expression, accountability, open government and informed participation.

### Start small

**1. Source census and partnerships**  
Map publishers, residents' groups, official feeds, datasets and archives.

**2. Read-only Southall prototype**  
A chronological civic timeline with source labels, topic filters and links to originals.

**3. Structured democracy**  
Connect reporting and community material to council meetings, documents and decisions, ideally using Open Council Network data.

**4. Personal subscriptions**  
Allow people to follow a town, ward, committee, topic or development through web, RSS or email without behavioural profiling.

**5. Conversation**  
Only once the information service is useful, explore an open discussion layer such as RSS.chat.

**6. Civic memory**  
Connect present issues to archived campaigns, previous decisions and historic local publishing.

### Who we want to work with

Potential partners include:
- Open Council Network and civic-tech projects;
- local journalists and publishers;
- residents' associations;
- community organisations;
- Ealing-wide civic groups;
- open-data specialists;
- accessibility and inclusion practitioners;
- local historians and archivists;
- technologists;
- researchers and funders interested in local democracy.

Participation would not imply endorsement of every other contributor or viewpoint.

### What success looks like

The first question is not "How many users did we acquire?"

It is:

**Did somebody understand their community better because these sources were connected?**

Other measures include whether residents discover information they otherwise would have missed, whether original council documents are used, whether smaller publishers gain useful reach, whether journalists and campaigners find relevant evidence faster, and whether users trust the service's attribution.

### An invitation

The internet made it easy for almost anybody to publish while somehow making it harder to understand what is happening where we live.

The information is already being created.

Councils publish. Journalists report. Campaigners investigate. Residents organise. Community organisations communicate. Archives remember.

The missing piece is the connection between them.

Southall & Ealing Civic Commons proposes to begin in one community, connect what already exists, build openly, preserve the original sources and find out whether that simple idea can make local democracy easier to see, understand and participate in.

**We would like partners to help us test it.**
